Everything After the Build

AI made building an app easy. It did nothing for the part where an app becomes a business. That gap is the company.

Build → Publish → Monetize → Bill → Operate → Grow

// AI collapsed the first box. The other five are still weeks of unfamiliar, high-friction work — and where almost every solo builder stalls.

The thesis

The problem was never “write the code”

A developer can now get a working app out of an AI tool in a weekend. Then they hit a completely different wall — one AI hasn’t touched:

Publishing means App Store Connect and Play Console: app records, builds, metadata, screenshots, pricing, tax and banking agreements, content ratings, review, staged rollout, and handling rejection. The AI wrote the code. It doesn’t become the developer account, prepare everything correctly, submit it, and get the app live.

Monetization is a design problem in disguise: free vs. paid, subscription vs. one-time, trial length, price by country, paywall design, what stays free, upsells, win-back. Apple’s own subscription system has a dozen lifecycle states before you’ve made a rupee.

Billing is an operations problem: receipt validation, entitlements, renewals, billing retries, refunds, cancellations, dunning, invoices, tax, multi-currency, web-and-mobile sync. This is the entire reason RevenueCat exists — and even RevenueCat is a set of tools you still have to assemble and understand.

So the future AI is creating is asymmetric: millions of people who can build an app, and very few who can turn one into a business. We sell into that asymmetry.

Positioning

Sell the outcome, not another SDK

The market is full of excellent point solutions — RevenueCat for billing infrastructure, App Store Connect for commerce, growth agencies for the rest. Every one of them hands the developer a part and leaves the assembly as an exercise.

The pitch

Not “here’s an SDK for subscriptions.” Instead: “Give us your app. We get it published, monetized, and making money.” The developer’s job stays build and improve the product. Our job is turn the product into a business.

Business model

Free to launch, paid on results

An indie developer with zero revenue will not pay a monthly SaaS fee before they know the app works. That objection kills adoption. So we don’t charge one.

Alignment

Free / low-cost to launch. The platform takes a percentage of revenue — roughly developer 85% / platform 15%, tiered by how much we operate (Stage 1 only vs. full growth partner). An optional flat fee later for high-revenue apps that prefer it. We earn only when the developer earns.

The one hard problem

The billing crux

Store payouts go straight to the developer’s bank account, tied to the developer’s tax identity. We can’t skim our 15% at the source. How we legally and reliably collect a revenue share is the single biggest risk in the model — and it gets validated before anything is built.

Likely hybrid — to be confirmed with a CA + lawyer

Store-sourced revenue: developer authorizes a payment mandate (UPI AutoPay / card / debit). We read reconciled revenue from the App Store Connect & Play financial reports, invoice the agreed %, and auto-charge — with clear ToS and service-pause on default.

Web-sourced revenue: we host the web checkout as merchant of record, take our cut at source, remit the rest. Cleanest path where it applies.

Open questions: agent-invoicing vs. merchant-of-record structure; India GST, TDS, and FEMA / RBI treatment of inbound remittance. If there’s no clean answer, the model needs rework first.

Constraints

Three walls that shape the architecture

1 · Apps publish under the developer’s own accounts

Apple Guideline 4.2.6 and Google Play’s spam / repetitive-content policies forbid one umbrella account mass-shipping apps. We hold delegated API access (Play service-account user; App Store Connect API key) and automate the setup so the friction is hidden. Because each app is a genuine, distinct product, 4.2.6 risk is low — this is managed Fastlane + CI, not a template farm.

2 · Store billing is mandatory for digital goods

Trials, subscriptions and digital unlocks must use Google Play Billing / Apple IAP — 15% under $1M/yr via the small-business programs, 30% above, subs dropping to 15% after 12 months. Only physical goods and real-world services may use an outside processor.

3 · The account holder carries all liability

Privacy policy, India DPDP / GDPR data-safety disclosures, content rating, refunds, DMCA — all sit with whoever’s account publishes. The compliance pack is generated and maintained per app, on the platform’s side.

Before heavy build

What to validate first

The billing crux. With a CA + lawyer, confirm how the platform collects a revenue share on store-sourced income, and the India GST / TDS / FEMA treatment. No clean answer → rework the model before anything else.

Policy validation. Open real Google Play + Apple developer accounts; read the current Developer Program Policies and Guideline 4.2.6; confirm the delegated-access permission model on both consoles.

Manual pilot. Take 3 friendly developers with real AI-built apps end to end by hand — accounts, build, test, compliance, submit, monetize, dashboard. Measure time, cost, and every rejection reason. The most important step.

Monetization loop. Ship one pilot app with free trial → subscription via RevenueCat; confirm trial, conversion, store commission, payout, and revenue-share collection all work end to end.

Demand test. A landing page with the “everything after the build” pitch and revenue-share pricing; measure signups from indie-dev communities before building Stage 2.
